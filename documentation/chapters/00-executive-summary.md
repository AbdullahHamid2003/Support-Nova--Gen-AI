# Executive Summary

Manual complaint handling makes an agent decide what each message is about, how urgent it is, who owns it, which policy applies, whether to escalate and what may be promised. The SupportNova Software Requirements Specification (theme ResponseX Intelligence, category Generative AI PowerPlay) names the consequences: inconsistent classification, delayed routing, missed escalations, inappropriate responses and incomplete resolutions. It also rules out the shortcut of sending complaint text to a Generative AI model and displaying the answer.

SupportNova is a web-based complaint-intelligence system built for Lumora Home Technologies, a fictional smart-home electronics company with 15 products and services, 10 departments and a taxonomy of 11 complaint categories and 35 subcategories. It uses Python (FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, PostgreSQL 17) and a React 19 TypeScript interface. One principle governs it: **GenAI proposes. Python validates. Ground truth decides.**

Two sources of approved knowledge support every decision. The **Knowledge Base** holds 24 policy documents in 29 versions (PDF, DOCX and Markdown), parsed into 484 traceable chunks that keep the document ID, version, section and page reference. Only Active versions within their effective dates count as evidence, and documented precedence rules settle conflicts between policies, SOPs and FAQs. The **Complaint Resolution Rule Matrix** is written as version-controlled YAML and copied into the database, where every change is validated, versioned and audited. Its 276 rule rows include 116 resolution rules with required and prohibited actions and eligibility outcomes, 39 escalation rules and 35 routing rules. The Knowledge Base supplies the evidence; the Rule Matrix supplies the decision criteria.

Each complaint is normalised, screened for prompt injection and stripped of personal data, and a hybrid lexical and vector search retrieves active policy sections. The **GenAI Complaint Intelligence Pipeline (Pipeline 1)** sends the complaint, verified order facts and this evidence to OpenAI gpt-4.1-mini (Anthropic and Gemini adapters are configurable). The model returns a strict 37-field JSON analysis covering classification, urgency, priority, routing, policy citations, resolution steps, eligibility, escalation, clarification questions and agent guidance. Invalid answers are logged and retried a fixed number of times.

The **Python Ground-Truth Validation Pipeline (Pipeline 2)** never calls a model. It works out its own expected outcome from deterministic signal detection, the order ledger and the Rule Matrix, then runs 52 checks across 12 dimensions. The **Comparison Engine** records, field by field, the AI proposal and the rule requirement (the "AI vs rules" view). Where the two disagree, the rules set the department, urgency, priority, escalation level, eligibility and required actions. A second GenAI call writes the customer response from this validated decision. Python then checks the draft for unsupported promises and timelines, untraceable amounts, tone and sensitive data.

A weighted score over the check results, together with 14 manual-review triggers, decides the outcome. A case is either **Verified** (score of at least 80, no critical failure, no trigger) or sent to **Manual Review** with its reasons. Reviewers can approve, reject, modify, reclassify, reassign, escalate, regenerate the response or comment, and both the original and the final decision are kept.

Complaint text reaches the model only inside a nonce-tagged block marked as untrusted, and document sections that contain embedded instructions are quarantined. Five roles with 27 permissions are enforced on the server, passwords are hashed with bcrypt, and the API key never leaves the server. Every action is written to an append-only, hash-chained audit log. Role-based dashboards, trend alerts and ten report types, exportable as PDF, Excel and CSV, cover the rest of the complaint lifecycle.

The measured results come from a synthetic dataset of 617 development complaints and 154 unseen holdout complaints. On the holdout set, the AI got at least one of six key fields (category, subcategory, department, urgency, priority, escalation level) wrong in 93 cases. In 92 of them (98.9%), Python either supplied the correct value or sent the case to manual review. Python's key-field accuracy was 82.7%, against 79.2% for the AI. All six prompt injections in the holdout set were detected with no false positives, and all 18 scenarios in the adversarial lab met their expectations. The automated suite of 206 backend and 12 frontend tests passes without calling a paid API.

The limits are measured too. The full pipeline takes 21.7 seconds at the median and 33.9 seconds at the 95th percentile, so only 32.4% of complaints finish within the 20-second target. The validated decision is ready after about 16 seconds, but it is saved together with the response draft. The manual-review share is high: 71% of the analysed development complaints and 84% of the holdout cases. Safety and privacy cases always need a person, and the model often leaves out a required action or under-rates urgency, which the rules then correct. Scaling to 10,000 complaints has been designed for but not load-tested. The public deployment, demonstration video and technical blog are still outstanding. Chapters 35, 43 and 44 discuss these limits.
