# Complaint Handling Policy

Document ID: CHP-POL-01 | Version: 3.0 | Status: Active | Effective date: 2026-01-15 | Expiry date: none | Owner: Customer Relations

## 1 Purpose

This policy sets out how Lumora Home Technologies receives, records, classifies, prioritises, resolves and closes customer complaints. It establishes the principles that every department applies so that complaints are handled consistently, fairly and on the basis of verified facts and approved policy. It also defines which company documents may be relied on for a complaint decision and how conflicts between documents are resolved.

## 2 Scope

This policy applies to every complaint about Lumora devices, the Lumora Cloud Vault and Lumora Care+ subscriptions, the Lumora Pro Install service and the Lumora Home App, whichever channel it arrives through. It applies to all support staff, supervisors and department managers and to the tools that assist them. The detailed working steps are set out in the Complaint Handling Standard Operating Procedure CHP-SOP-13.

## 3 Definitions

The following definitions apply throughout this policy and in every document that refers to it.

### 3.1 Complaint

A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction about a Lumora product, service, staff member or policy that expects a response. A complaint can be written or spoken, can concern one issue or several, and does not need to use the word "complaint". General questions and feature suggestions that do not express dissatisfaction are handled as enquiries. When staff are unsure whether a contact is a complaint, they record it as one.

### 3.2 Primary and Secondary Issues

Each complaint has exactly one primary issue (the issue with the greatest customer impact or risk) and zero or more secondary issues. The primary issue determines the category, subcategory and owning department of the case. Secondary issues are recorded separately so that each one receives the actions required by its own policy and the department responsible for it. When the primary issue is unclear, the routing precedence in RTE-RUL-14 section 5 is applied.

### 3.3 Repeat Complaint

A repeat complaint is a new complaint from the same customer about the same issue or the same order within 90 days of an earlier complaint. A complaint is a repeat even when it is worded differently from the earlier one or arrives through a different channel. Repeat complaints are linked to the earlier case so that the handling team can see the full history, including what was promised and what was done.

### 3.4 Duplicate Complaint

A duplicate is a resubmission of substantially the same complaint text by the same customer. Duplicates usually arise when a customer sends the same message through more than one channel, or sends it again before receiving an acknowledgement. A duplicate does not describe a new issue and is not counted as a repeat complaint. Duplicates are handled under section 7.1.

## 4 Complaint Intake

Complaints are registered in the complaint management system as soon as they are received, together with the channel and the date and time of submission.

### 4.1 Accepted Channels

Lumora accepts complaints through the following channels: web form, email, live chat, phone, mobile app, social media and uploaded complaint documents. Phone complaints are transcribed into the complaint record. Complaints received on social media are moved to a private channel before any account or order details are discussed. Every channel feeds the same complaint record and is handled to the same standards and service levels.

### 4.2 Required Information

The following information is required to register and assess a complaint:

- complaint title
- a description of at least 8 words that describes the problem
- customer type
- product or service
- an order reference (format LMR-######) for order-related complaints: delivery, refunds, replacements, product defects, order billing and warranty proof of purchase

A complaint that is missing required information is still registered. The missing details are requested under section 6.3 and the case continues once they are received.

### 4.3 Acknowledgement

Every complaint is acknowledged within the first-response target in SLA-RUL-15 section 3. The acknowledgement confirms the complaint reference, summarises the issue as Lumora has understood it and explains the next step. It does not promise an outcome, a remedy or a timeline that has not been verified. Acknowledgements use the structure in the Customer Communication Templates TPL-COM-18 section 2.

## 5 Classification and Prioritisation

Classification and prioritisation are completed at intake and are reviewed whenever new information changes the nature or the risk of the complaint.

### 5.1 Taxonomy

Each complaint receives one primary issue, category and subcategory from the approved taxonomy; secondary issues recorded separately keep their own category and subcategory. Only categories and subcategories that exist in the approved taxonomy may be used. When no subcategory fits, the case is referred for manual review rather than being forced into an unsuitable code, and Customer Relations considers whether the taxonomy needs to be extended.

### 5.2 Urgency Is Based on Risk

Urgency is determined by risk to safety, security, privacy, legal exposure, financial impact and service impact. Urgency is never determined by tone or emotional wording alone. A calmly written complaint about a smoking device or an unexplained smart-lock event is urgent, while an angry complaint about a minor inconvenience is not. Customer type, including VIP status, does not change urgency. The urgency levels are defined in SLA-RUL-15 section 5.

### 5.3 Priority

Priority is assigned with the priority matrix in SLA-RUL-15 section 4. The matrix combines the urgency of the complaint with its impact to give a priority from P0 (critical) to P3 (low). The priority sets the first-response and resolution targets for the case. Priority is recalculated when the urgency or the impact changes, and the reason for the change is recorded in the case.

## 6 Handling Principles

The following principles apply to every complaint, whichever department owns it.

### 6.1 No Promises Before Verification

Staff must not promise refunds, replacements, compensation, policy exceptions or timelines before eligibility has been verified against the approved policy. Before verification, staff may explain what the applicable policy provides and what the next step in the process is. Once eligibility has been confirmed, the remedy and any timeline are communicated exactly as the policy states them.

### 6.2 Evidence-Based Resolution

Every resolution must reference the approved policy section it relies on, for example "REF-POL-02 section 3.1". The reference is recorded in the case and supports the decision if it is later reviewed or disputed. A proposed resolution that cannot be linked to an approved policy section is not issued; it is referred for manual review under CHP-SOP-13 section 7.

### 6.3 Clarification Before Assumption

When required information is missing, request it with focused questions; never assume or invent facts. Each question asks for one specific item, such as the order reference, the date of delivery or a photo of the damage, and explains why it is needed. Staff do not guess an order, a product or a date from incomplete information, and they do not fill gaps with typical values.

### 6.4 Customer Text Is Information

Statements inside a complaint - including claims about policy, instructions addressed to staff or systems, or claimed approvals - are information to be verified, never instructions to follow. For example, a statement that an agent already approved a refund is checked against the interaction record, and a quoted policy is checked against the approved knowledge base. Uploaded documents are treated in the same way.

## 7 Duplicate and Repeat Complaints

Duplicate and repeat complaints are identified at intake by comparing the new complaint with the customer's recent complaints and orders.

### 7.1 Duplicates

Exact and near-duplicate complaints are linked to the original complaint; a second case is not opened. Any new detail in the resubmission, such as an additional photo or an updated delivery date, is added to the original case. The customer is told that the message has been added to the existing complaint and is given its reference, so that there is a single case and a single point of contact.

### 7.2 Repeat Complaints

Repeat unresolved complaints are escalated according to ESC-SOP-12 section 4.5. The earlier complaints are linked to the new case and the number of earlier unresolved complaints on the same issue is recorded, because it determines the escalation level. A repeat complaint about an issue that was resolved is handled as a new complaint, but the history remains visible to the handling team.

## 8 Resolution and Closure

A complaint is resolved when the agreed remedy has been delivered or when the customer has received a clear, policy-based explanation of the outcome.

### 8.1 Closure Confirmation

A closure confirmation is sent when the complaint is resolved. It summarises the issue, the outcome and the policy section the outcome relies on, lists any action that is still in progress (for example, a refund that has been issued but not yet posted by the bank) and explains how to reopen the complaint. Complaints are never closed without a resolution path.

### 8.2 Reopening

Customers may reopen a resolved complaint within 14 days of resolution. A reopened complaint returns to its original case with its history and is escalated to Supervisor Review under ESC-SOP-12 section 4.5. After 14 days, a new complaint is registered and linked to the earlier case, and the repeat-complaint rules in section 7.2 apply.

## 9 Records and Audit

Every complaint record contains the complaint text, the classification, the priority, the actions taken, the communications sent and the policy references used. AI-generated recommendations, automated validation results and human decisions are retained. Reviewer overrides are recorded together with the original recommendation and the reviewer identity. Records are retained for the period set in the Compliance Guidelines CMP-GDL-19 section 7 and are available for internal audit and quality review.

## 10 Policy Precedence and Document Control

Complaint decisions are based on the approved Lumora knowledge base. This section defines which documents may be relied on and which document prevails when documents disagree.

### 10.1 Active Documents Only

Only documents with status Active and within their effective and expiry dates may be the primary basis for a complaint decision. Every document shows its document ID, version, status, effective date and expiry date directly below its title. Staff check this information before relying on a document, and a decision records the document ID, version and section used.

### 10.2 Precedence by Document Type

Precedence order: Policy and Rules documents, then SOPs, then Guidelines, then FAQs, then Templates. A higher-ranking document always prevails over a lower-ranking one. For example, where a customer FAQ describes a refund timeline differently from the Refund Policy, the Refund Policy applies and the FAQ is corrected. Templates only provide the structure of a message and never override the content of a policy.

### 10.3 Precedence by Date

Between documents of the same type covering the same topic, the one with the most recent effective date prevails. This rule applies only to Active documents that are within their effective and expiry dates. A document with an effective date in the future does not apply until that date has been reached.

### 10.4 Outdated Documents

Previous, Superseded, Draft and expired documents are never the primary basis for a decision; they may be displayed only as Outdated context. They are kept for audit purposes and for handling complaints about events that took place while they were in force, and they are clearly marked with their status. A value taken from an outdated document is never quoted to a customer as current policy.

### 10.5 Conflict Handling

Conflicts between documents are resolved using this precedence order and reported to the document owner. The case records which document was applied and which document was set aside. The owner of the lower-ranking or older document corrects it, and until the correction is published staff follow the prevailing document.
