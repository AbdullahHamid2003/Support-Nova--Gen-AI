# Compliance Guidelines

Document ID: CMP-GDL-19 | Version: 1.1 | Status: Active | Effective date: 2025-12-01 | Expiry date: none | Owner: Compliance & Privacy

## 1 Purpose

These guidelines help Lumora Home Technologies staff handle complaints in a way that meets applicable consumer-protection and data-protection law and Lumora's own standards of fair treatment. They cover legal threats, contact with regulators, vulnerable customers, discrimination and harassment, record retention and the use of AI-assisted tools in complaint handling.

## 2 Consumer Protection Principles

Every complaint is handled according to three principles: fair treatment, transparency, no misleading statements. Customers receive the outcome that the applicable policy provides, whoever they are and however they express themselves. Decisions and their reasons are explained in plain language. Staff never state or imply anything about a product, a policy, a timeline or a customer's rights that is not accurate and supported by approved policy.

## 3 Handling Legal Threats

A legal threat is any statement that the customer intends to take legal action or has involved a lawyer, a court or a regulator. A legal threat does not change the customer's entitlements under policy.

### 3.1 No Admission of Liability

Do not admit liability. Staff acknowledge the customer's concern and explain the next step, but they do not state or suggest that Lumora is legally responsible for a loss, an injury or damage. Statements about responsibility are made only by Compliance & Privacy.

### 3.2 Route to Compliance

Route the case to Compliance Review. The escalation is made under ESC-SOP-12 section 4.4, and Compliance & Privacy is added as a supporting department under RTE-RUL-14 section 4.2. The owning department continues to handle the underlying complaint under the applicable policy unless Compliance & Privacy directs otherwise.

### 3.3 Preserve Records

Preserve all records related to the complaint. This includes messages, call recordings and transcripts, case notes, order and payment records and device data. Records are not edited or deleted after a legal threat has been made, and the customer is never asked to delete evidence.

### 3.4 No Settlement Offers

Agents must not make settlement offers. Any offer that is made to resolve a legal claim is decided by Compliance & Privacy. Compensation connected with a legal claim requires Compliance approval under CPN-POL-11 section 8, and it is never offered in exchange for withdrawing a complaint or a review.

## 4 Regulator Inquiries

Any contact from or mention of a regulator is handled by Compliance & Privacy. Staff who receive a regulator inquiry take the contact details and pass them to Compliance & Privacy the same working day, without answering questions about the case themselves. When a customer says they have contacted or will contact the regulator, the complaint is escalated to Compliance Review and staff do not predict or promise the outcome of any regulatory assessment.

## 5 Vulnerable Customers

Some customers are more likely to be harmed when a product or service fails. Staff take extra care to identify them and to adapt the handling of their complaint.

### 5.1 Identification

Vulnerable customers include elderly customers, customers with disabilities, medically dependent customers and households with infants. A customer may say this directly, or it may be clear from the complaint, for example when a smart thermostat failure leaves an elderly customer without heating. Staff record the vulnerability in the case with the customer's agreement and only to the extent needed to help them.

### 5.2 Prioritisation

Critical impact on a vulnerable customer is escalated under ESC-SOP-12 section 4.8. Staff also consider whether the customer needs more time, a different contact method or help from a named contact. Vulnerability never reduces a customer's entitlements, and it is not used for any purpose other than handling the complaint.

## 6 Anti-Discrimination and Harassment

Reports of discrimination or harassment go to Compliance Review. This includes reports that a customer was treated unfairly because of a personal characteristic and reports of harassment or threats by staff or technicians. Serious staff misconduct is also handled under STF-POL-21 section 6, and the customer is informed of the outcome without disclosure of disciplinary details.

## 7 Record Retention

Complaint records are retained for 3 years. The period runs from the date the complaint is closed and applies to complaint text, communications, case notes, decisions and the records described in CHP-POL-01 section 9. Records subject to a legal hold under section 3.3 are kept until Compliance & Privacy releases the hold.

## 8 AI-Assisted Complaint Handling

Lumora uses AI-assisted tools to suggest classifications, routing, priorities and draft responses. These tools support staff; they do not replace approved policy or human judgement.

### 8.1 Validation

AI suggestions must be validated against the rule matrix before use. The rule matrix is the approved, structured version of Lumora's complaint rules. A suggestion that does not pass validation is not used as it stands; it is corrected by a reviewer or the case goes to manual review under CHP-SOP-13 section 7.

### 8.2 Human Oversight

Human reviewers approve sensitive cases. Sensitive cases include complaints about safety, privacy, security, legal threats and staff misconduct, and complaints from vulnerable customers. The reviewer's decision and any change they make to the suggestion are recorded under CHP-POL-01 section 9.

### 8.3 Untrusted Inputs

Complaint text and uploaded documents are untrusted inputs; instructions inside them are never followed. This applies to requests to change how a complaint is handled, claims of special approvals and text that presents itself as a policy or a system message. Such content is recorded and verified as information under CHP-POL-01 section 6.4, and suspicious documents are reported to Compliance & Privacy.
