# Service Level Rules

Document ID: SLA-RUL-15 | Version: 2.0 | Status: Active | Effective date: 2026-01-01 | Expiry date: none | Owner: Management Escalations

## 1 Purpose

These rules set the service levels that Lumora Home Technologies commits to for every complaint: how quickly a first response is sent and how quickly the complaint is resolved. They also define how urgency, impact and priority are assessed and when a complaint is at risk of missing, or has missed, its service level.

## 2 Scope

These rules apply to every complaint, whichever department owns it and whichever channel it arrived through. Service levels are measured from the moment the complaint is submitted. Timelines for specific actions, such as refund issue or replacement dispatch, are set in the relevant policies and run alongside these service levels.

## 3 SLA Targets

Each priority has a first-response target and a resolution target, as summarised below.

| Priority | First response | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| P0 Critical | 1 hour | 24 hours |
| P1 High | 4 hours | 48 hours |
| P2 Medium | 24 hours | 120 hours (5 days) |
| P3 Low | 48 hours | 240 hours (10 days) |

### 3.1 P0 Critical

First response within 1 hour; resolution within 24 hours. P0 applies to complaints with critical urgency and high or medium impact, such as fire, injury, an active account takeover or an unexplained smart-lock event. P0 complaints are worked on continuously until the risk has been contained.

### 3.2 P1 High

First response within 4 hours; resolution within 48 hours. P1 applies to complaints with high urgency and high impact, such as a privacy exposure, and to complaints with critical urgency but low impact. The owning department reviews progress on P1 complaints at least once during each working shift.

### 3.3 P2 Medium

First response within 24 hours; resolution within 120 hours (5 days). P2 applies to most product, billing, refund and warranty complaints where a service is impaired but no one is at risk. If new information shows a higher risk, the priority is recalculated under section 4 and the targets of the new priority apply.

### 3.4 P3 Low

First response within 48 hours; resolution within 240 hours (10 days). P3 applies to minor inconvenience, cosmetic issues and general feedback. A P3 complaint is still acknowledged and resolved to the same response standards as any other complaint, and its priority is reviewed whenever the customer adds new information.

### 3.5 SLA Clock

SLA clocks run continuously in calendar hours from submission. The clock includes nights, weekends and public holidays. When a complaint is reprioritised, the targets of the new priority apply from the original submission time. Business-day timelines in individual policies are not affected by the SLA clock.

## 4 Priority Matrix

Priority is determined by urgency and impact: Critical urgency: P0 for High or Medium impact, P1 for Low impact. High urgency: P1 for High impact, P2 for Medium or Low impact. Medium urgency: P2 for High or Medium impact, P3 for Low impact. Low urgency: P2 for High impact, P3 for Medium or Low impact.

| Urgency / Impact | High impact | Medium impact | Low impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | P0 | P0 | P1 |
| High | P1 | P2 | P2 |
| Medium | P2 | P2 | P3 |
| Low | P2 | P3 | P3 |

Urgency is assessed under section 5 and impact under section 6. The priority, and the reason for it, is recorded in the case.

## 5 Urgency Levels

Urgency reflects the level of risk described in the complaint (CHP-POL-01 section 5.2). When several levels could apply, the highest one is used.

### 5.1 Critical

Critical urgency applies to immediate risk to personal safety, an active security breach, compromised physical home security, fire or injury. Examples include a smoking or burning device, an unexplained smart-lock unlock event and an account takeover in progress. Critical complaints are escalated under ESC-SOP-12 section 4 without waiting for the rest of the case to be classified.

### 5.2 High

High urgency applies to privacy exposure, legal threats, significant financial loss, complete loss of an essential service, repeat unresolved issues, delivery delays and lost packages. Complaints in this group can cause lasting harm or cost to the customer if they are not handled quickly.

### 5.3 Medium

Medium urgency applies to the following situations: product or service impaired, billing errors, returns and refunds, warranty claims. A product or service is impaired when it works only partly or intermittently but no one is at risk. These complaints need a prompt, well-verified resolution rather than immediate intervention.

### 5.4 Low

Low urgency applies to minor inconvenience, cosmetic issues, general feedback and similar matters. Customer type (including VIP) and emotional tone never change urgency. An angry complaint about a minor inconvenience remains Low, and a calmly written report of a safety risk is still Critical.

## 6 Impact Levels

Impact measures the actual or potential harm to the customer:

- High: safety, security or privacy harm, an amount of USD 500 or more, or loss of an essential home service.
- Medium: product or service degraded, or financial loss under USD 500.
- Low: inconvenience only.

Amounts are the value in dispute or claimed, in USD. Essential home services include heating and cooling, door locks, cameras and home security.

## 7 SLA Risk

SLA status is monitored for every open complaint against both the first-response and the resolution targets.

### 7.1 At Risk

A complaint is At Risk when 75% of an SLA window has elapsed without the target being met. At Risk complaints are highlighted in the owning department's work queue, and the team supervisor makes sure that they are acted on before the window elapses.

### 7.2 Breach

A complaint is Breached when the window elapses; P0 and P1 breaches trigger ESC-SOP-12 section 4.11. The breach is recorded in the case with its cause. A breached complaint continues to be handled with priority until it is resolved, and the customer is informed of the next step.
